# Speech (谨言)

Speech is the most immediately consequential virtue. Words, once spoken or written, cannot be unsaid. They travel faster than thought, outlast the moment, and shape how others perceive you, your ideas, and your intentions. Careful speech is not about being diplomatic or calculating — it is about ensuring that what you say is true, necessary, and kind. The filter is not "can I say this?" but "should I say this, and in this way?"

The discipline of speech operates in three stages: before, during, and after. Before speaking, ask whether the words are true, whether they are necessary, and whether they are helpful. During speaking, listen to yourself — not to monitor your performance, but to notice when you are speaking to fill silence, to impress, or to deflect rather than to communicate. After speaking, review: did the words accomplish what you intended? Were they received as you meant them? This is not self-censorship. It is self-awareness.

In professional contexts, the cost of careless speech is amplified. A poorly worded email can derail a project. A thoughtless comment in a meeting can undermine a colleague's confidence. A hasty Slack message can be screenshotted and forwarded. The digital context makes speech even more consequential because it creates a permanent record. Before you type, ask: "Would I be comfortable if this appeared in a document reviewed by everyone I respect?" If not, rewrite it.

## Daily Practices

- Pause for two seconds before responding to provocative or surprising statements
- Before sending written communication, re-read it once from the recipient's perspective
- When you do not know something, say "I don't know" instead of constructing a plausible-sounding answer
- Avoid speaking about others in ways you would not say to their face
- Replace gossip with direct conversation — if it is worth saying, it is worth saying to the right person

## Reflection Questions

- Did anything I said today create confusion, harm, or misunderstanding that I could have prevented?
- Was I speaking to contribute or to be heard?
- What conversation am I avoiding that would be more productive if I had it directly?
