# 利他 (Altruism)

Altruism is the virtue of serving others without expectation of return. It is not self-sacrifice in the sense of neglecting your own wellbeing — that is unsustainable and ultimately serves no one. Rather, it is the recognition that your flourishing is connected to the flourishing of those around you. The most functional systems are not those where each individual maximizes their own gain, but those where individuals contribute to the collective health of the whole.

The practical expression of altruism is contributing your unique abilities to problems larger than yourself. This could be mentorship, sharing knowledge, building tools that help others, or simply showing up consistently for the people who depend on you. The key is that the contribution is genuine — not performed for recognition or reciprocity. True altruism is invisible because it does not demand to be seen. It is the pull request merged without fanfare, the advice given without expectation, the time spent helping someone who cannot help you back.

The counterintuitive truth is that altruism is not a zero-sum game. Helping others succeed does not reduce your own chances — in a functioning ecosystem, it increases them. Knowledge shared is knowledge multiplied. Opportunities extended to others create networks of trust that benefit everyone. The most successful people in any field are often those who have contributed the most value to others, not those who have extracted the most value for themselves.

## Daily Practices

- Find one person you can help today without expecting anything in return
- Share knowledge publicly — write, teach, mentor
- When you see a problem that affects others, ask "what can I contribute to solving this?"
- Practice active listening: sometimes the most altruistic thing is giving someone your full attention
- Notice when you are helping for recognition and gently redirect toward genuine contribution

## Reflection Questions

- Who did I help today, and did I expect anything in return?
- How am I contributing to the community around me?
- What knowledge or skill do I have that could benefit someone else if shared?
- Am I taking more from my community than I am giving?
