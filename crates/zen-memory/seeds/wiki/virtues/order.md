# Order (秩序)

Order means that everything has a place, and everything is in its place. It is not about tidiness for its own sake — it is about reducing friction. When your workspace is organized, your files are findable, your schedule is structured, and your commitments are tracked, you spend less cognitive energy on logistics and more on the work that matters. Disorder is a silent tax on every action you take throughout the day.

The practical application of order is twofold: physical and temporal. Physical order means your workspace, your files, and your tools are organized so you can find what you need without searching. Temporal order means your time is allocated deliberately — you know what you are working on and when, rather than drifting between tasks based on urgency or whim. Both require maintenance. Order degrades naturally. Without active maintenance, entropy wins.

The most common objection to order is that it feels constraining. "I work better in creative chaos." This is usually a rationalization for the effort required to maintain order. Creative work benefits from structure more than routine work because creative work requires sustained focus, and chaos fragments attention. The constraint of order is not a constraint on creativity — it is a constraint on distraction. You are free to think deeply precisely because you do not have to think about where things are.

## Daily Practices

- At the start of each day, identify the three most important tasks and allocate specific time blocks for them
- At the end of each day, return your workspace to a clean state — clear desk, organized files, no loose ends
- Maintain a single capture system for all commitments — do not scatter notes across multiple places
- Once a week, review and purge: delete old files, archive completed projects, clear your inbox
- Use consistent naming conventions and folder structures for digital files so you can find things without searching

## Reflection Questions

- Did I know where everything I needed was today, or did I spend time searching?
- Am I reacting to the day's demands, or did I decide in advance what matters most?
- What one organizational change would save me the most time this week?
