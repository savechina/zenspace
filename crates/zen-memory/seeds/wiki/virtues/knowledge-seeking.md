# 求知 (Knowledge-Seeking)

Knowledge-seeking is the virtue of continuous intellectual growth. It is not about accumulating facts but about deepening understanding — building better mental models, connecting ideas across domains, and staying curious about how the world works. The求知 person reads not to confirm what they already know but to discover what they do not know. They approach every field as a student, even the one they have mastered.

The practice of knowledge-seeking is daily and systematic. It means reading across disciplines, not just within your specialty. It means engaging with ideas that challenge your assumptions. It means maintaining the humility to say "I don't know" and the discipline to follow that with "but I will find out." Charlie Munger's latticework of mental models is the result of a lifetime of cross-disciplinary learning — psychology, economics, physics, biology, history — each field providing a different lens for understanding complex problems.

The danger of neglecting this virtue is intellectual stagnation. Without active learning, your mental models become brittle. You apply the same frameworks to every problem, even when they no longer fit. The world changes, but your understanding stays frozen at the point you stopped learning. Knowledge-seeking is the antidote to this freeze — the practice of updating your understanding as the world reveals new information.

## Daily Practices

- Read something outside your field every day — even fifteen minutes shifts your perspective
- After learning something new, try to explain it to someone else; if you cannot explain it simply, you have not understood it
- Keep a list of questions you cannot answer — this is your learning frontier
- When you encounter a strong opinion, seek out the strongest counterargument
- Review what you believed a year ago and note how it has changed

## Reflection Questions

- What did I learn today that I did not know yesterday?
- When did I last change my mind about something important?
- What question am I currently curious about, and what am I doing to answer it?
- What blind spots might I have in my current understanding?
