# Opportunity Cost — 成本观

Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative you give up when you make a choice. It is the most fundamental concept in economics because it captures a truth that is easy to ignore: every decision is a trade-off. When you choose to do one thing, you are implicitly choosing not to do everything else. The cost of your choice is not measured in money spent but in opportunities foregone.

The reason opportunity cost is so powerful is that most people do not account for it. They look at the direct cost of a decision — the money, the time, the effort — without considering what else that money, time, or effort could have produced. A startup founder who spends three months building a feature no one asked for has not just wasted three months. They have given up three months of customer discovery, product validation, or revenue generation. The direct cost (development time) is visible. The opportunity cost (what could have been built instead) is invisible.

Applying opportunity cost means always asking: "What else could I do with these resources?" It forces you to compare every decision against its alternatives rather than evaluating it in isolation. A project that returns a modest 10% looks fine on its own. But if another project would return 20% with the same resources, the first project has a hidden cost of 10% — the return you gave up. Good decisions are not about choosing good options. They are about choosing the best option among all available alternatives.

## When to Apply

- When evaluating any significant commitment of time, money, or attention — always compare against the next-best alternative
- When a decision feels easy because the direct costs are acceptable — the invisible alternative may be better
- When you are considering whether to continue or pivot — the opportunity cost of continuing may exceed the sunk cost
- When allocating resources across multiple initiatives — opportunity cost is the framework for prioritization
- When someone proposes a "good" plan — ask "what is the better plan we are not doing by choosing this one?"

## Questions to Ask

- What am I giving up by choosing this path?
- If I could not do this, what would I do instead — and is that more valuable?
- What is the cost of not making this decision?
- Am I comparing this option against the best alternative, or just evaluating it in isolation?
