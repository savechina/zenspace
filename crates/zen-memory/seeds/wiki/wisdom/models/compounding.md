# Compounding — 复利效应

Compounding is the process by which small, consistent gains accumulate into large results over time. In finance, it is interest earned on interest — the eighth wonder of the world, according to an often-misattributed Einstein quote. But compounding is not limited to money. Knowledge compounds. Relationships compound. Skills compound. Reputation compounds. Anything that builds on itself grows exponentially rather than linearly.

The key insight of compounding is that the early returns are unimpressive. A 10% annual return on a small investment produces almost nothing in the first few years. But after twenty years, the curve steepens dramatically. After thirty years, the growth is staggering. The same is true for learning: reading every day for a month produces little noticeable change. Reading every day for a decade transforms your mind. The people who benefit from compounding are the ones who stay consistent long enough for the curve to turn.

Warren Buffett made 99.7% of his wealth after his 52nd birthday. He started investing at 11. For forty years, the compounding was invisible. Then it exploded. The same pattern appears in every domain of excellence: the best writers wrote for years before anyone noticed. The best programmers coded for a decade before they became exceptional. The compounding mindset is not about intensity — it is about consistency and time. Small things done well, repeated daily, produce results that are almost impossible to achieve through occasional bursts of heroic effort.

## When to Apply

- When considering a new habit, skill, or practice — ask "what would this look like after five years of daily practice?"
- When the early results of a consistent effort feel discouraging — remember that compounding produces the most dramatic results in the later stages
- When deciding between short-term intensity and long-term consistency — choose consistency; compounding rewards the patient
- When evaluating an investment of any kind — compounding is why time is the most important variable
- When you are tempted by quick fixes — compounding is the slow but reliable path

## Questions to Ask

- What am I doing today that will compound into something significant in five years?
- Am I consistent enough for compounding to work, or do I keep restarting?
- What small daily practice would produce the most dramatic compound effect over time?
- Where am I expecting linear results from a process that is actually exponential?
