# Circle of Competence

Your circle of competence is the set of things you genuinely understand well enough to make good decisions about. It is defined as much by its edges as by its center. Knowing what you do not know — and being honest about it — is more valuable than pretending expertise you lack.

Buffett and Munger attribute much of their success to two disciplines: identifying their circle of competence and staying inside it. This is harder than it sounds. The pressure to have an answer, to appear competent, or to avoid admitting ignorance pushes people past their edges. Once outside the circle, you are guessing. Guessing in domains with real consequences is how people lose.

The practical skill is distinguishing between "I understand this" and "I have heard of this." Reading a blog post about machine learning does not put neural networks in your circle. Implementing a production system does. The gap between awareness and competence is where overconfidence lives. A useful test: if you cannot explain why a decision is correct to someone who disagrees, you may be outside your circle.

## When to Apply

- When asked to make a decision in an unfamiliar domain — say "I don't know" or find someone who does
- When evaluating whether to delegate — delegate tasks outside your circle to those whose circle includes them
- When you notice yourself rationalizing a decision with vague reasoning — you may be operating past your edge
- Before taking on new responsibilities — honestly assess whether this falls within your competence
- When someone asks for your opinion on something you have only surface-level knowledge of
