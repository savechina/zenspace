# 情绪冲动 (Emotional Impulse)

Emotional impulse is making a significant decision while in a heightened emotional state — anger, excitement, fear, or euphoria — without a cooling-off period. Emotions compress time perception and amplify certain factors while suppressing others. The angry email gets sent. The exciting purchase gets made. The fearful exit gets executed. In each case, the decision feels urgent and correct in the moment, but the urgency is manufactured by the emotion, not by the situation.

The neurological reality is that strong emotions suppress prefrontal cortex activity — the brain region responsible for deliberative reasoning. You are literally thinking with a less capable brain when emotional. The 24-hour rule is not arbitrary caution; it is a protocol for waiting until the neurochemistry returns to baseline. Decisions made in emotional states have measurably worse outcomes because they are made with impaired hardware.

## How It Manifests

- Sending a confrontational message within minutes of receiving provocation
- Making a large purchase during a moment of excitement or social pressure
- Quitting a job or relationship in anger, then regretting it within days
- Accepting or rejecting an offer based on how it feels right now rather than on analysis
- The decision feels "obvious" and "urgent" — both are red flags

## Warning Signs

- You feel a strong push to decide NOW rather than later
- Physical symptoms: elevated heart rate, shallow breathing, muscle tension
- The language in your internal monologue is absolute: "must," "never," "always"
- You feel relief at the prospect of deciding quickly — relief is not analysis
- Someone who knows you well would be surprised by this decision

## Counter-strategy

- For any decision above a cost threshold, enforce a mandatory 24-hour waiting period
- Write down the decision and the reasoning — re-read it the next day
- If the reasoning still holds when calm, proceed with confidence
- If the urgency disappears overnight, the emotion was driving the decision
- Track emotional decisions and their outcomes — build personal evidence of the pattern

## Related Principles

- 二阶思维 (Second-Order): "and then what?" — emotions suppress second-order analysis
- 奥卡姆剃刀 (Occam's Razor): is the simplest explanation for your urgency an emotion?
