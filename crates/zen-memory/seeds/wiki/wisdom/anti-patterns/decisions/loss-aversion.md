# 损失厌恶 (Loss Aversion)

Loss aversion is the tendency to continue investing in a failing course of action because of resources already committed. The sunk cost fallacy is its mechanism: past investment feels like a reason to continue, when economically it is irrelevant to the forward decision. What matters is only whether future returns justify future costs. But the brain does not compute expected value — it computes emotional attachment to what has already been spent.

The asymmetry is brutal. Every additional unit of investment in a failing direction increases the emotional attachment while decreasing the probability of recovery. The more you lose, the harder it becomes to stop. This is why gamblers double down, why founders burn through life savings, and why organizations fund doomed projects for years past the point of rational exit. The stop-loss line exists to be triggered mechanically, without negotiation, precisely because the moment of greatest loss is the moment of weakest judgment.

## How It Manifests

- "We've already invested six months — we can't stop now"
- Adding more capital to a losing position to "average down"
- Continuing a project because abandoning it feels like admitting failure
- Keeping a failing hire because of the onboarding investment
- The decision to stop is always "one more quarter" away

## Warning Signs

- Your argument for continuing references past investment rather than future returns
- You feel that stopping would waste the effort already spent
- The rational exit point was identifiable months ago but keeps moving
- You are negotiating with yourself about whether the stop-loss "really" applies
- Emotional language dominates: "give up," "admit defeat," "throw away"

## Counter-strategy

- Pre-commit to a stop-loss line BEFORE investing — write it down, share it with someone
- When the stop-loss triggers, execute mechanically — do not negotiate
- Reframe: stopping is not wasting the past investment — it is protecting the remaining capital
- Extract sustained value before exiting: what can be salvaged, repurposed, or learned?
- Track the "one more quarter" pattern — if you've said it twice, you're in the trap

## Related Principles

- 成本观 (Cost Awareness): distinguish sunk costs (irrelevant) from future costs (relevant)
- 永远有筹码 (Keep Chips): stop-loss preserves chips for the next opportunity
