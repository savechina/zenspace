# 达克效应 (Self-Cognition Block / Dunning-Kruger)

The self-cognition block is the gap between perceived competence and actual competence. Specifically, it is the condition where confidence exceeds 0.9 while historical decision accuracy is below 0.5. The Dunning-Kruger effect describes this precisely: low-ability individuals lack the metacognitive skill to recognize their own incompetence, leading to inflated self-assessment. They are not lying about their confidence — they genuinely cannot see the gap.

This pattern is especially dangerous because high confidence feels like strength. It feels like clarity, decisiveness, and leadership. But confidence without calibration is just noise. The truly dangerous combination is high confidence paired with low humility — the decision-maker who not only believes they are right but also refuses to track outcomes that might prove otherwise. Without outcome tracking, there is no feedback loop, and without a feedback loop, confidence can only increase, never correct.

## How It Manifests

- Strong opinions held with absolute certainty on topics with limited track record
- Dismissing contrary evidence as "exceptions" or "different circumstances"
- Making predictions without recording them, so accuracy can never be measured
- Reacting defensively to feedback rather than curiously
- Confidence has increased over time but no outcome data has been collected

## Warning Signs

- You feel more than 90% confident but cannot cite specific evidence
- You have not tracked your past predictions against actual outcomes
- Feedback from others feels like an attack rather than data
- Your confidence has never decreased — that means it has never been tested
- You cannot recall a recent decision where you were proven wrong

## Counter-strategy

- Track every significant decision: what you predicted, what actually happened, confidence level
- Compute humility score: ratio of correct predictions to total predictions made
- If humility score < 0.5 but confidence > 0.9, FORCE a confidence recalibration
- Actively seek disconfirming evidence — assign someone to challenge your assumptions
- Practice intellectual humility: "I might be wrong about this" should be a default stance
- Require minimum 10 tracked decisions before high-confidence claims are trusted
