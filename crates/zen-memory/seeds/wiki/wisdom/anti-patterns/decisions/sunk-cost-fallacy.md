# 沉没成本谬误 (Sunk Cost Fallacy)

The sunk cost fallacy is the tendency to continue investing in a course of action because of past investment — time, money, or effort — rather than because it represents the best path forward. The fallacy is rooted in a deeply human aversion to waste. Abandoning a project feels like admitting that the time already spent was wasted. But continuing a failing project does not recover the past investment — it only adds new losses to the old ones.

The mechanics are straightforward but powerful. You have invested significant resources in a project, relationship, or strategy. The evidence suggests it is not working. But the thought of walking away triggers a visceral sense of loss — not the loss of future opportunity, but the loss of everything already invested. Your brain processes this as: "If I stop now, all that was for nothing." The correct calculation is: "Everything I have already spent is gone regardless. The only question is whether spending more will produce a better outcome than spending it elsewhere."

The most dangerous aspect of sunk cost is that the greater the investment, the harder it is to walk away. This is precisely backward: the more you have invested, the more important it is to ensure the remaining investment is well-directed. Large sunk costs should increase your scrutiny, not decrease it. The question is never "how much have I put in?" but rather "what is the best use of my resources from this point forward?"

## How It Manifests

- Continuing a failing project because "we have already invested six months"
- Holding a losing stock because "I don't want to sell at a loss" — the market does not know your purchase price
- Staying in a bad relationship because of the years already invested
- Reading a boring book to the end because you are halfway through
- Eating food you do not want because you already paid for it

## Warning Signs

- You justify continuation with past investment rather than future prospects
- You catch yourself thinking "after all I have put into this..."
- Someone asks "if you were starting fresh today, would you make the same choice?" and the answer is no
- You are tracking how much you have invested as a reason to continue
- The emotional weight of stopping feels heavier than the rational weight of continuing

## Counter-strategy

- Make the decision as if starting from zero: "If I had not already invested anything, would I start this today?"
- Separate the past from the future: the past is gone, the future is all that matters
- Frame the choice as between two futures, not between a future and a past
- Ask someone who has no emotional investment in the outcome — they will see the sunk cost clearly
- When you notice yourself tracking past investment as a decision factor, name it: "this is the sunk cost fallacy"

## Related Principles

- 机会成本 (Opportunity Cost): every dollar spent continuing is a dollar not spent on better alternatives
- 永远有筹码 (Margin of Safety): preserving future optionality is more important than recovering past losses
