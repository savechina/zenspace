# 惯性思维 (Inertia Thinking)

Inertia thinking is the assumption that what worked before will work again, without verifying that the conditions that made it work still hold. It is the cognitive shortcut of extrapolating from a sample size of past successes while ignoring that the environment may have changed. The strategy that generated growth last year may be actively destroying value this year because the market, the competition, or the technology has shifted underneath you.

This pattern is particularly dangerous for experienced decision-makers. Success creates confidence, and confidence reduces the impulse to verify. The more times a strategy has worked, the less likely you are to question it. But each success also increases the probability that competitors have studied your playbook. Past success is not evidence of future success — it is evidence of past conditions that happened to favor your approach.

## How It Manifests

- "This is how we've always done it" as the primary justification for a strategy
- Continuing to invest in a declining channel because it worked for three years
- Repeating a pricing strategy after a major competitor entered the market
- Using last year's marketing playbook despite a shift in customer behavior
- Assuming a hiring process that worked at 10 people will work at 100

## Warning Signs

- Your primary argument for a strategy is a historical success story
- You cannot articulate what conditions made the past strategy work
- You have not checked whether those conditions still hold
- Environment signals (market shift, new competitor, regulatory change) are dismissed
- The word "proven" is used to shut down questioning rather than invite scrutiny

## Counter-strategy

- For every strategy, list the assumptions that must hold for it to work
- Independently verify each assumption against current data — not historical data
- Ask: "If I were starting fresh today with no history, would I still choose this approach?"
- Track leading indicators, not just lagging indicators — detect environment shifts early
- When past success is cited, ask: "What has changed since then?"

## Related Principles

- 第一性原理 (First Principles): strip to fundamentals — do the conditions still apply?
- 二阶思维 (Second-Order): what happens when competitors adapt to your strategy?
